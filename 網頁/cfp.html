Conference theme

About the theme

Welfare states stand at a crossroads. Artificial intelligence is remaking labour markets and care systems, while climate change compels countries to pursue net zero and to rethink the social risks that transition brings. Added to these are the pressures of super-ageing and ultra-low fertility, and the gaps in social safety nets exposed by the pandemic. What social policy faces is no longer the repair of a single programme but the reconstruction of a paradigm.

The conference takes the coming of an AI society as its first theme and eco-social policy under the environmental transition as its second. It places both within the structural context of rapid demographic transition around the world, and draws them together around two lines of policy response: social investment and social innovation.

Awards

Best Paper Awards for PhD students and early career researchers

EASP presents Best Paper Awards for PhD students and early career researchers. There are two awards.

Postgraduate Award

For current PhD students.

Early Career Researcher Award

For researchers who received their PhD within the past six years.

How to be considered

To be considered for the EASP Postgraduate Award or the EASP Early Career Researcher Award, applicants must meet the above criteria and submit a full paper for review by the EASP Postgraduate Award Committee by 1 June 2027. Applicants must be the sole or first author of a paper accepted for the conference programme. When submitting your abstract, please indicate if you wish to be considered for the Award. The winners will be announced at the conference.

Awards

FISS Han Emanuel Best Paper Prize

In 2011 FISS introduced the Best Paper Prize competition. In collaboration with Policy Press, the prize is awarded to the best, previously unpublished paper presented at each year’s FISS conference. The winning paper is published in the Journal of Poverty & Social Justice. Selection and publication are subject to a refereeing process. In addition, the prize winner will receive a personal subscription to the Journal for 2 years.

The winning paper is selected by members of the FISS Board of Governors who draw up an initial short-list of papers presented at the conference. The authors of the short-listed papers are asked whether they would like to be considered for the prize, and any potential revisions will be discussed at this stage. A deadline for submitting the papers for consideration will then be set, and the final decision will be made by a committee of FISS Governors. The winning paper will be subject to the Journal’s normal peer review processing prior to publication.
