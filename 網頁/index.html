About

About the conference

The East Asian Social Policy research network (EASP) brings together researchers, policymakers, and practitioners with a shared interest in social policy and welfare across East Asia. Since its founding, EASP has fostered comparative dialogue on pensions, health and long-term care, family policy, labour markets, inequality, and the changing role of the welfare state.

The 2027 conference is the 23rd EASP International Conference, held jointly with the 33rd International Conference of the Foundation for International Studies of Social Security (FISS). It is hosted by the Department of Social Work, National Taiwan University, in Taipei.

The conference takes the coming of an AI society as its principal axis and eco-social policy under the environmental transition as its second. It places both within the structural context of East Asia's demographic transition, and draws them together around two lines of policy response: social investment and social innovation.

Conference theme

Between Algorithms and Net Zero

In Search of a New Social Policy Paradigm

East Asian welfare states stand at a crossroads. Artificial intelligence is remaking labour markets and care systems, while climate change compels countries to pursue net zero and to rethink the social risks that transition brings. Added to these are the pressures of super-ageing and ultra-low fertility, and the gaps in social safety nets exposed by the pandemic. What East Asian social policy faces is no longer the repair of a single programme but the reconstruction of a paradigm.

Principal axis

The Coming of an AI Society

Automation and the platform economy are rewriting employment, leaving social protection built around standard employment relationships out of step. AI also brings new instruments to social services and care — from care technology and risk prediction to administrative automation — and with them new questions of digital divides, data governance and algorithmic accountability.

Second axis

Eco-social Policy

The drive towards net zero redistributes jobs and social risks. How can a just transition ensure that vulnerable groups are not left behind, and how can ecological sustainability be built into the design of the welfare state? East Asian economies depend heavily on export manufacturing and have low energy autonomy — reconciling growth, decarbonisation and social protection calls for answers of the region's own.

Structural context

East Asia's Demographic Transition

Taiwan became a super-aged society in 2025, fertility in South Korea keeps setting new global lows, and Japan's population continues to shrink. Ageing and ultra-low fertility squeeze the financing and workforce of pensions, health care and long-term care — and interlock with both axes above, from care shortages that make care technology unavoidable to the transition's employment effects on older workers.

Policy responses

Social Investment & Social Innovation

Social investment turns the welfare state from compensation after the fact towards prevention and capacity-building earlier in the life course. Social innovation stresses collaboration among the state, market, social organisations and communities. Eight years after the 2019 Taipei conference took up productivism and social investment, EASP 2027 returns to take stock — and extends the view to social innovation under AI and ecological transition.
